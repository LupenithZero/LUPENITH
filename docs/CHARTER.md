# Draft Charter

**Version:** 0.1-draft  
**Status:** Open for criticism; not legally binding and not yet adopted by a registered organization.

This charter describes a proposed global, science-oriented, humanist community focused on independent thinking, responsible freedom, mutual support, and open technology. The text is a starting point, not sacred or beyond criticism.

## Preamble

Human beings deserve the tools, knowledge, relationships, and practical opportunities to shape their lives with dignity. Evidence can correct mistaken beliefs; cooperation can solve problems that no person can solve alone; and accountable institutions can reduce the risk that power becomes domination.

This initiative aims to help people become more capable and independent without teaching contempt for people who need help. It values resilience without cruelty, freedom without coercion, and community without unquestioning obedience.

## Article 1 — Human dignity and equal standing

1. Every person has equal basic moral worth, regardless of rank, wealth, ability, nationality, language, sex, disability, belief, or relationship to the founder.
2. A rank may describe a demonstrated skill or a temporary responsibility. It never establishes a person's greater human worth.
3. No person should be treated as property, a tool, or a means to another person's ambitions.

## Article 2 — Evidence, uncertainty, and intellectual honesty

1. Claims about the observable world should be evaluated according to evidence, sound reasoning, and the quality of available methods.
2. The community will distinguish established findings, reasonable hypotheses, personal values, and unknowns.
3. Members must be free to question claims, including the founder's claims, the charter, majority opinions, expert conclusions, and AI outputs.
4. Changing one's mind after receiving better evidence is a strength, not a betrayal.
5. No supernatural claim is required for participation. The organization will not assert divine authority as the basis of its public rules. Personal religious or non-religious beliefs remain private and voluntary, provided members respect others' rights.

## Article 3 — Freedom of conscience and exit

1. Participation is voluntary. No person must worship, profess a belief, pledge personal loyalty, or accept a supernatural explanation.
2. Members may criticize, disagree, abstain, pause participation, or leave without threats, harassment, retaliation, or coercive pressure.
3. The organization must not encourage members to isolate themselves from family, friends, independent advice, or outside communities.
4. Access to lawful personal choices, essential services, and rights outside the organization must not be controlled by the organization.

## Article 4 — Autonomy and mutual support

1. The initiative should help people develop practical skills, critical thinking, health literacy, economic capability, and the confidence to make informed decisions.
2. Personal responsibility includes recognizing constraints, seeking help when useful, and addressing harms one causes. It does not mean blaming people for every hardship or ignoring structural barriers.
3. Mutual aid should aim to expand the recipient's choices and capacity, not create dependency or demand obedience.
4. No person should be shamed for illness, disability, poverty, trauma, or needing professional assistance.

## Article 5 — Freedom with boundaries

1. Members may hold different opinions and lead different lawful personal lives.
2. Freedom does not justify violence, coercion, fraud, harassment, exploitation, non-consensual conduct, or deliberate violation of another person's rights.
3. Intervention in another person's choices must have a legitimate reason grounded in preventing serious harm or protecting rights; it should be proportionate, lawful, reviewable, and no more intrusive than necessary.
4. Private thoughts, doubts, identity, harmless disagreement, and lawful personal choices are not misconduct by themselves.

## Article 6 — Privacy and data minimization

1. The initiative should collect the least personal information necessary for a clearly stated purpose.
2. Sensitive personal information must not be made public merely in the name of transparency.
3. Access to member information should be restricted by role, logged where appropriate, and removed when no longer necessary.
4. Any future service must explain its data practices honestly, including known limitations and the party responsible for operating it.

## Article 7 — Accountable power

1. No founder, leader, expert, donor, elected majority, committee, or AI system is above the charter, criticism, or fair process.
2. Authority must be limited by written duties, term limits where applicable, conflict-of-interest rules, transparent records, and independent review.
3. A person accused of misconduct must receive notice of the allegation, a fair opportunity to respond, an impartial review, a proportionate outcome, and a route of appeal.
4. Good-faith criticism and reporting of wrongdoing must not be punished as disloyalty.

## Article 8 — Open knowledge and technology

1. The initiative should prefer open standards, auditable processes, accessible documentation, and suitable open-source licenses.
2. Contributions must respect third-party licenses, privacy, security, and intellectual-property rights.
3. No product may be described as secure, private, scientifically validated, or production-ready without evidence proportionate to the claim.
4. Building a new tool is not automatically better than improving, supporting, or responsibly adopting an existing one.

## Article 9 — Social and economic integrity

1. The initiative will not promise jobs, loans, profits, therapy, shelter, or other benefits that it cannot currently provide.
2. No one should be pressured to donate, pay membership fees, recruit others, perform unpaid labor, or surrender personal assets to demonstrate loyalty.
3. Any future financial or social program must publish eligibility rules, funding sources, conflicts of interest, risks, and audit arrangements as appropriate.
4. Aid, education, and child-support services must not be conditional on religious belief, recruitment, or obedience.

## Article 10 — Protected principles and amendments

The following protections are foundational to the identity of this initiative and may not be removed by an ordinary vote while the organization continues to operate under this charter:

- equal basic dignity and freedom of conscience;
- the right to criticize and leave voluntarily;
- protection from coercion, abuse, and retaliation;
- fair process and the right to appeal serious decisions;
- no unilateral final punitive authority for a leader or AI;
- privacy and protection of personal information;
- no compulsory belief, worship, recruitment, or personal loyalty;
- no deprivation of basic membership rights merely for rank or dissent.

Other provisions may be amended through the published governance process. A proposal to remove a protected principle must not be handled as an ordinary amendment. It requires independent constitutional review, a long public notice period, and an explicit decision on whether the organization should dissolve or reconstitute under a different charter. No leader or AI may bypass this requirement.

## Article 11 — Evidence of success

The initiative should assess success by independently reviewable outcomes, such as:

- whether contributors and participants gain useful knowledge or skills;
- whether products solve real problems and treat users fairly;
- whether governance decisions can be understood and challenged;
- whether the organization corrects mistakes and protects dissent;
- whether social programs produce meaningful, safe, and sustained benefits.

Membership count, donations, public attention, or loyalty to a person are not sufficient measures of success.

## Article 12 — Limitations and review

This charter is an ethical and governance proposal, not legal advice or evidence that the organization has been legally established. Before any real-world organization, financial service, healthcare-related activity, or child-support program begins, the relevant sections must be reviewed by qualified professionals for the applicable jurisdiction.

## Proposed review questions

1. Which provisions are ambiguous or unrealistic?
2. What abuse cases are not covered?
3. How could the founder misuse this charter despite its stated protections?
4. How should disagreements between local law and the charter be handled without putting members at risk?
5. What measurable outcomes would show that the initiative helps people become more capable and free?
