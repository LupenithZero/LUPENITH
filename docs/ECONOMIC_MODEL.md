# Long-Term Economic and Social Model

**Status:** Concept only. No business, employment network, loan program, charity, child-care service, or support fund currently exists. The initiative is not soliciting money.

The long-term aim is to fund useful public and member benefits through sustainable, lawful economic activity rather than dependence on public donations. This is an aspiration, not a promise of income or services.

## 1. Principles

- Separate organizational governance from commercial incentives.
- Never promise jobs, loans, grants, shelter, profits, or financial returns before a real program and funds exist.
- Do not pressure members to buy products, donate, recruit, invest, or work without agreed terms.
- Use written budgets, conflict disclosures, transparent eligibility rules, independent accounting, and appropriate tax and legal review.
- Pay people fairly where work is paid; clearly label volunteer work and its limits.
- Do not use access to community, social support, or personal data as leverage to sell products.
- Avoid speculative financial schemes, member recruitment-based compensation, and investment promises.

## 2. Potential earned-revenue activities

Subject to proof of demand and legal review, possible activities include:

- Open-source support, consulting, and maintenance services.
- Training and educational products with accessible free materials.
- Paid services built around open technology, without withholding necessary security fixes from users as leverage.
- Cooperative businesses in which participating workers have clear rights and transparent accounts.
- Grants or institutional contracts only if their conditions are compatible with the charter and accepted transparently. The initial preference is not to depend on public donations.

None of these activities has launched. The first practical step is to identify one small service that can be delivered honestly with existing skills and capacity.

## 3. Possible allocation of future surplus

Only after costs, wages, taxes, contractual duties, reserves, and legal obligations are met, a future organization could publish a policy for allocating surplus among:

- Product and infrastructure development.
- Training and scholarships.
- A transparent emergency assistance program.
- Child and youth support delivered through qualified, lawful, independently safeguarded partners or programs.
- Reserves for continuity and security.

Do not choose percentages before there is reliable revenue, an operating budget, and member review.

## 4. Employment and skills network

A future employment network could publish verified opportunities, transparent selection criteria, skill-development pathways, and fair treatment standards. Participation must remain voluntary, and members must be able to pursue opportunities outside the organization.

A person's rank, belief, or loyalty should not replace job-relevant qualifications. Employers must not receive private member data without a clear lawful basis and the person's informed agreement.

## 5. Loans and financial support

Loans require careful legal and financial design. Before any lending, the organization would need to determine licensing requirements, affordability assessments, default handling, interest or fee policies, privacy safeguards, conflicts of interest, and independent oversight. Never lend money that the organization cannot afford to lose, and never guarantee a loan before funds and legal authority exist.

An emergency support program should use public eligibility criteria, confidentiality, conflict management, a right to appeal, and a process that does not require ideological conformity.

## 6. Child and youth support

The priority should be children's safety, development, voice, family and community connections where safe, education, and future autonomy—not expanding the organization's influence. Programs must comply with local law, involve qualified professionals, and be independently safeguarded. Institutional care should not be the default where safe family-based alternatives are available.

No child should be required to adopt the organization's philosophy, perform work for its businesses, or remain a member in the future to receive care or educational support.

## 7. Separation and oversight

If commercial entities and social programs are created, they should have clear legal identities, separate accounts, written service agreements, public summaries, conflict-of-interest controls, and independent audits appropriate to their size. A leader must not unilaterally move funds among entities or use charitable resources for personal benefit.

## 8. Financial transparency template

Any future operating entity should publish, at a suitable level of detail:

- Reporting period and legal entity.
- Revenue categories and major expenditure categories.
- Wages and related-party payments in an appropriately privacy-preserving way.
- Restricted funds and their permitted purposes.
- Conflicts of interest and related-party transactions.
- Independent review or audit status.
- Outcomes achieved and limitations.
