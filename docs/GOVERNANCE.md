# Proposed Governance Model

**Status:** Discussion draft. No election, council, rank system, or binding AI decision process currently exists.

The purpose of governance is to coordinate shared work while preventing concentrated power. Procedures should be understandable, auditable, accessible across languages, and safe for dissenters.

## 1. Equal basic membership rights

Every eligible member should have equal basic standing in general elections. Skill certifications and service roles may expand what tasks someone is qualified to perform, but must not create extra votes or greater human worth.

Membership should be voluntary and based on a clear, public agreement to basic conduct rules. Avoid collecting identity documents unless a specific, lawful, proportionate need has been established.

## 2. Separate responsibilities

As the organization grows, consider separating these functions:

- **General membership:** proposes initiatives, elects representatives, discusses major changes, and may challenge decisions.
- **Elected executive council:** coordinates approved projects and budgets within a written mandate.
- **Independent review panel:** handles appeals, allegations involving leadership, conflicts of interest, and procedural complaints.
- **Technical and professional reviewers:** advise within defined areas of expertise but do not control the whole organization.
- **AI-assisted analysis:** summarizes proposals, checks consistency, identifies risks, and helps make records searchable. It does not replace democratic authority or independent review.

These bodies should not all be controlled by the same person. Any future charter must define appointment, removal, rotation, and conflict-of-interest rules.

## 3. Founder and leadership protections

The founder may help establish the initial direction but must not receive permanent or unlimited authority. Proposed safeguards for discussion:

- A defined leadership term, proposed initially as two years.
- A limit of two consecutive terms for the same executive role, subject to review by members.
- Public criteria for candidacy and disqualification, with an appeal route.
- Published agendas, decisions, budget summaries, and conflict-of-interest declarations, with privacy and safety exceptions documented.
- An independent route for complaints about the founder or executives.
- No power for a leader to change the process used to investigate or remove that same leader.
- No AI-generated score alone may disqualify a candidate or remove a leader.

### Protecting against malicious or frivolous removal attempts

A removal process should require a documented allegation, a defined threshold for initiating review, an independent assessment, notice to the person concerned, a fair chance to respond, and a member decision where appropriate. Serious immediate risks may justify temporary, reviewable suspension of a specific authority—not automatic guilt.

Do not make removal practically impossible. A system that protects a leader from all accountability is not secure governance. Exact thresholds should be tested before adoption and should consider geographic diversity, membership size, accessibility, and the risk of coordinated harassment.

## 4. Elections and scoring

If candidate scoring is used, it should evaluate relevant and verifiable qualifications against published criteria. It should not rank a person's moral worth or political loyalty.

- Publish scoring rubrics and weights before applications open.
- Allow candidates to review and challenge factual errors.
- Disclose who scores candidates and any relevant conflicts of interest.
- Provide a human review and appeal route.
- Separate minimum role qualifications from the actual member vote.
- Keep one-person-one-vote for ordinary elections unless a carefully reviewed, lawful system establishes a different arrangement with a strong democratic justification.

## 5. Member ranks and certifications

Ranks should be skill or role certifications, not a permanent social caste. A draft model:

- **Participant:** can read, discuss, and contribute without taking an exam.
- **Charter-literate member:** has completed a free, accessible assessment on the charter and conduct rules.
- **Practitioner or specialist:** has demonstrated a specific skill through transparent criteria.
- **Mentor or reviewer:** is qualified to support or assess a particular field.
- **Elected officer:** holds temporary authority through a defined mandate and election or appointment process.
- **Independent reviewer:** is selected through a process designed to minimize conflicts of interest.

Safeguards:

- Free study materials and reasonable accommodations.
- Multiple languages and accessible formats.
- Retakes and an appeal process.
- No extra general-election votes based on rank.
- No humiliation or loss of essential community access for low rank.
- No required pledge of loyalty to the founder or ideology beyond agreement to conduct rules.
- Publish what a certificate means—and what it does not mean.

## 6. Decision lifecycle

For consequential proposals, use a visible sequence:

1. **Proposal:** state the problem, purpose, expected benefit, affected groups, cost, and alternatives.
2. **Evidence:** publish sources, uncertainties, likely risks, and arguments against the proposal.
3. **Consultation:** provide a clear comment period and accessible summary.
4. **Conflict review:** disclose financial, personal, or organizational interests.
5. **Decision:** use the method specified for that type of decision; record the result and rationale.
6. **Implementation:** assign an accountable owner, budget, timeline, and success criteria.
7. **Evaluation:** assess results against those criteria.
8. **Revision or rollback:** correct failures and record what was learned.

Emergency decisions should be narrow, time-limited, logged, and reviewed afterwards.

## 7. Constitutional amendment process

Protected principles in the charter cannot be removed by an ordinary vote. For ordinary amendments, the eventual voting system should include advance notice, public rationale, a consultation period, a supermajority for constitutional-level changes, and an auditable record.

A proposed starting point—not an adopted rule—is at least 30 days of public review and a two-thirds majority of valid votes for major amendments. A longer period and stronger process may be needed as the membership grows. No vote should be accepted as legitimate if coercion, vote buying, fabricated identities, or material manipulation has been demonstrated.

## 8. Disputes, sanctions, and appeals

Responses should be based on conduct and evidence, not belief or personal dislike. Use the least restrictive response that adequately protects people and the project. Possible measures include explanation, mediation, correction, warning, temporary removal of a specific privilege, or removal from a role or community after fair process.

No private punishment, public doxxing, forced confession, collective retaliation, or punishment of family members is permitted. Serious threats or crimes should be handled through appropriate lawful channels rather than an improvised internal tribunal.

## 9. Transparency with privacy

Publish governance rules, non-sensitive decision records, aggregate finances, and amendment history. Do not publish private identities, health information, private messages, security details, or unverified allegations merely to appear transparent.

## 10. Minimum viable governance for the pilot

At the pilot stage, do not simulate a complex global parliament. Use a public issue tracker, a short written proposal template, transparent maintainer decisions, a published conflict-of-interest statement, and a documented appeal route. Reassess the design after real people have used it.
